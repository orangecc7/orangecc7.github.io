Overview

Agentic systems are transforming Web search, data analysis, and scientific discovery through workflows that combine reasoning, memory, and tool use. As agents take on more complex tasks, managing their state, execution, and interactions becomes essential to keeping results reliable, costs manageable, and decisions traceable.

This workshop explores these challenges through database research and graph data mining. Database techniques offer foundations for efficient workflows, consistent state, and auditability; graph and geometric methods support structured memory, retrieval, coordination, and reasoning. We also examine capability boundaries and safe, verifiable self-improvement. Through invited talks, paper and demo presentations, and panel discussions, researchers and practitioners will exchange methods and define a research agenda for reliable and accountable Web intelligence.

Call for Papers

Submission details to be announced.

We invite research papers, short or position papers, and demo contributions on the management of agentic systems. Topics include, but are not limited to:

  1. Agent workflow infrastructure

    declarative interfaces, query and workflow optimization, execution engines, resource-aware tool use, checkpoints, recovery, and reproducible pipelines.

  2. State, memory, and provenance

    persistent agent state, consistent memory updates, temporal knowledge, memory consolidation, provenance tracking, versioning, and decision auditability.

  3. Graph-grounded agentic systems

    graph-based retrieval, knowledge graphs, GraphRAG, evidence graphs, task dependency graphs, tool graphs, and graph-structured agent memory.

  4. Multi-agent coordination and governance

    shared knowledge, concurrent updates, communication graphs, delegation, controlled tool access, policy enforcement, and human oversight.

  5. Geometric and representation learning for agents

    Riemannian, hyperbolic, and other geometric representations for hierarchical memory, relational retrieval, structured reasoning, and complex graph-structured evidence.

  6. Scientific and data-intensive workflows

    hypothesis and experiment tracking, data preparation, validation, reproducibility, iterative refinement, and agent-assisted discovery over structured and semi-structured data.

  7. Evaluation, benchmarks, and limits

    capability boundaries, failure analysis, robustness, safety constraints, trustworthy evaluation, benchmark design, and trade-offs among accuracy, cost, latency, and controllability.
